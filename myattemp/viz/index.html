Conversion panel explorer

train.csv · Jan–Nov 2026 · every rate is a monthly conversion rate: of the clients present in a month, the share that converts that month. New here? Start with Guide.

What one row is

One client in one calendar month. A client keeps appearing every month until the month they convert (objetivo = 1), then disappears for good. Nobody leaves for any other reason. The 8,061 who never convert are all still present in November.

The client's profile (age, income, products, risk band…) is identical in every month. Only dias_ultima_interaccion changes, and that change is injected noise (tab 6).

The one number used everywhere

Monthly conversion rate = conversions ÷ rows, for a group of rows. It is the probability that a client present in a month converts in that month, which is exactly what the competition asks for December. The overall value is 15.0%, drawn as a dashed line on every chart.

The only time-varying variable is being overwritten

Share of rows where dias_ultima_interaccion equals dias_ultima_transaccion, by calendar month. When different, it is uncorrelated random noise (r ≈ 0).

…so its predictive power decays month by month

Gini of each recency variable within each month. Transaction recency is stable; interaction recency fades as its copy gets replaced.

Frozen profiles

Share of multi-month clients whose value changes across their months. Age, account tenure and "days since last transaction" never move, so these are snapshots repeated each month, not monthly measurements.

Client types built only from the variables that carry signal

Risk band × product count × transaction recency × app activity. Bar = monthly conversion rate; the label shows the client count. The dashed line is the 15% average. "~N mo" = average months until conversion (1 ÷ rate).

What the column should be vs what it is

Should be: days since the last contact with the client. It should be ≤ days since last transaction (a transaction is a contact), and from month to month it should either reset or grow by about 30.
Is: a copy of dias_ultima_transaccion (itself frozen) in which each row is independently overwritten, with probability (month − 1) ÷ 11, by another client's transaction value from the same month. January 0% overwritten → November 91% → December 100%.

Six real clients present all 11 months

Gray line = their dias_ultima_transaccion (never changes). Dots = dias_ultima_interaccion: filled when equal to the transaction value (intact copy), hollow when overwritten. Overwritten values jump randomly; there is no "+30 per month" pattern.

Share of intact copies: observed vs the formula (12 − month) ÷ 11

The dots (observed) sit on the dashed line (formula) within 0.12 points every month, so the schedule is exact. Month 12 is December, the test month: 0% intact.

Does being overwritten change conversion?

Monthly conversion rate for rows with an intact vs an overwritten copy, within the same range of dias_ultima_transaccion. Identical, so the overwritten values carry no information about the target.

So its apparent predictive power is borrowed and fades

Gini per calendar month. Its whole-train Gini (−0.058, the 4th best variable) is the transaction signal leaking through intact copies. In December none remain. Dropping it costs nothing (LightGBM on November: 0.2322 without vs 0.2306 with).

The question

If this were real Banco de Crédito del Perú data, client groups would differ from each other (income by region, app use by age, iOS by income…). Their conversion would also react to the Peruvian calendar: gratificación in July (and December), CTS deposits in May and November, the school season in Feb–Mar, and mostly for formal (dependiente) workers. Each check below compares the expected real-world behavior with the data.

Fingerprint: 210 dependence tests between client attributes

Every pair of profile columns was tested for any relationship (Kruskal-Wallis, chi-square, Spearman). If columns are independent, p-values are spread evenly, about 10 per bar (dashed line), and about 5% fall below 0.05 by pure chance. Real bank data would pile up at the far left. Here the bars are flat.

Seasonality: observed ÷ expected conversion by calendar month

Expected = what the client profiles predict. Shaded = Peruvian pay-calendar months (Feb–Mar school, May/Nov CTS, Jul gratificación). The ratio drifts upward steadily (+1.7% per month in odds), with no peaks in the pay months.

Does any group have its own calendar?

Each line is one level of the group: observed ÷ expected conversion, after removing the common monthly level. With its own seasonality a line would leave 1.0 in its months (e.g. dependiente rising in May/Jul/Nov). Lines only scatter around 1.0, and the scatter is wider for small groups: that is sampling noise.

Expected in Peru vs observed

Benchmarks: StatCounter mobile OS share, Peru (Jun–Jul 2026); INEI 2024 labor income (Lima S/2,035, urban S/1,927, rural S/915 per month); Ley 27735 (gratificación Jul/Dec); CTS deposits May/Nov. Income currency in the data is unknown; only within-data contrasts are judged, not levels.

Do the conversion drivers differ between social groups?

Likelihood-ratio tests. "Main" = does the group shift conversion at all; "× drivers" = do risk band, products, recency, app and card act differently inside the group. Only the acquisition channel passes (p < 0.001); region's p = 0.04 does not survive 6 comparisons.

The one exception: high-risk clients acquired at a branch

Monthly conversion rate of high-risk clients by channel and by the 180-day transaction step. Branch-acquired high-risk clients convert about 4 points less on both sides of the step; dormant ones almost never convert. In low and medium risk, branch makes no difference.
